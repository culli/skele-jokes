SKELETON EYES: D6 ON EACH NANO ESP32

Both sketches now animate their own eyes while their own MP3 board reports
PLAYING. Both eyes share the D6 control output and flicker together. The effect
is generated by a timer; it does not measure the audio waveform or syllables.
It uses no delay(), so buttons, PIR, UART and Bluetooth keep running.

Upload motion_mp3/motion_mp3.ino to skeleton 1 and button_mp3/button_mp3.ino to
skeleton 2. Keep the new SkeletonEyes.h beside EACH sketch, along with the three
existing headers. In SkeletonEyes.h, EFFECT can be FLICKER (default), BLINK
(250ms on/off), or STEADY (on throughout playback). ACTIVE_LOW=true is for the
direct LED circuit and the PNP circuit below. D6 is the board label, independent
of pin-numbering mode.

Eyes are off during startup, idle, filename lookup, and the other skeleton's
turn. A received STOP/PAUSE state or a local skip/error turns them off. UART
status polling introduces a short delay relative to actual audio start/end;
long query retries can extend this. If playback status disappears entirely,
eyes go dark after 8 seconds without a valid status rather than blinking forever.

CONFIRMED PARTS

The eyes are bare LEDs with no attached resistors. They previously ran from a
three-AA (nominal 4.5V) battery box through a shared resistor. If that old
resistor's bands are brown-red-brown, it is 120 ohms. The available new resistors
are brown-black-red: 1k ohm. The photo looks like a gold tolerance band (+/-5%);
if silver, it is +/-10%. Either tolerance is suitable here.
The original 4.5V battery supply is not the LED's forward-voltage rating.
LED color, forward voltage and rated current remain unknown; start with the
low-current circuit below. Do not apply it to a different LED module/string.

FIRST TEST WITH THE EXISTING TWO-WIRE PARALLEL PAIR: NO TRANSISTOR

You can leave the wiring between the two LEDs intact. Use ONE shared 1k resistor
to deliberately limit the combined current to a few milliamps. With power off:

  Nano 3V3 -- 1k -- existing loose red (+) wire --+-- LED1 anode (+)
                                               +-- LED2 anode (+)

  Nano D6 -------- existing loose black (-) wire +-- LED1 cathode (-)
                                               +-- LED2 cathode (-)

Disconnect the old AA battery box, old shared resistor and any transistor
wiring. The black wire goes to D6, NOT GND. Use 3V3, NOT VBUS or the AA box.
The resistor must be in series with the red wire; do not bypass it. No transistor
is needed at this low current. Both existing sketches already support this
ACTIVE_LOW arrangement: D6 LOW lights the pair and D6 HIGH turns it off.

For red LEDs with about 2V forward drop, the shared resistor allows about
(3.3V - 2V)/1000 ohms = 1.3mA TOTAL, not 1.3mA per LED. Actual current depends
on LED forward voltage and GPIO voltage drop. Even if one LED takes almost all
the current, the shared 1k resistor keeps that current low. Leave the resistor
at 1k for this test; do not substitute the old approximately 120-ohm resistor.

The compromise is brightness: parallel LEDs do not necessarily share current
equally, so one may be dimmer or barely light. Separate resistors, as in the
next option, are preferable for predictable brightness. The earlier blanket
instruction against a shared resistor was too broad for this deliberately
low-current test. White/blue LEDs may also be dim at a 3.3V supply.

Optional: another 1k resistor between D6 and 3V3 holds the eyes off while the
Nano is resetting/booting. It adds about 3.3mA while D6 is LOW.

OPTION WITH A SEPARATE RESISTOR PER LED: NO TRANSISTOR

GuuYebe's product page/gallery was checked on October 17, 2026. It lists PNP,
60V, 0.6A, 0.4W and 200MHz but supplies no pinout diagram. Emitter/collector
orientation of the photographed GuuYebe 2N2907 A331 remains unverified.

For exactly two bare LEDs with a separate 1k resistor per LED, a transistor is
optional. The Nano can sink these small currents directly on D6. This circuit
works with the existing ACTIVE_LOW=true code; no firmware change is needed.

  Nano 3V3 -- 1k -- LED1 anode (+), cathode (-) --+
                                                +-- Nano D6
  Nano 3V3 -- 1k -- LED2 anode (+), cathode (-) --+

Optional: another 1k resistor between D6 and 3V3 holds the eyes off while the
Nano is resetting/booting. It adds about 3.3mA while D6 is LOW.

Disconnect the old battery box, old shared resistor and any PNP transistor
wiring before using this alternative. Here the joined LED negatives go to D6,
NOT GND. Each positive lead needs its own series 1k resistor. D6 LOW lights
both LEDs; D6 HIGH turns them off. The output is set HIGH at sketch startup.
Use only 3V3; do not connect this circuit to VBUS or the 4.5V battery box.

For a red LED with about 2V forward drop, each branch draws about 1.3mA:
(3.3V - 2V)/1000 ohms. Two branches are about 2.6mA total (about 5.9mA with
the optional pull-up), comfortably within the GPIO's low-level drive capability.
The actual current depends on LED forward voltage and GPIO voltage drop.
Brightness is limited by the 1k resistors. Keep those values for this test;
higher-current lighting should use a driver with a verified pinout.

ALTERNATIVE TRANSISTOR CIRCUIT: REQUIRES VERIFIED E/B/C PINOUT

The 2N2907 is a PNP transistor. Its base must not be directly GPIO-controlled
with its emitter at 5V/VBUS: a HIGH GPIO is only 3.3V, which cannot turn that
circuit off reliably and can expose the GPIO to excess voltage. The simple
direct-control example below uses 3V3 throughout. A higher-voltage load needs
a different driver circuit, sized for that load.

TRANSISTOR CONNECTIONS (A SEPARATE ALTERNATIVE TO DIRECT GPIO DRIVE)

Per skeleton: one 2N2907, four 1k-ohm resistors, two LEDs.
Use power OFF when making connections.
Disconnect the old battery box and old shared resistor from the eye circuit.
Use Nano 3V3 for this circuit, not VBUS or the 4.5V battery box.

  Nano 3V3 ------------------- E (emitter)
       |                         2N2907 PNP
       +---- Rpullup 1k ----+-- B (base)
                           |
  Nano D6 ------ Rbase 1k --+

                               C (collector)
                               |
                               +-- Rled1 1k -- LED1 anode (+)
                               |                cathode (-) -- Nano GND
                               |
                               +-- Rled2 1k -- LED2 anode (+)
                                                cathode (-) -- Nano GND

The collector feeds TWO separate resistor+LED branches in parallel. Each LED
has its OWN series resistor. Rbase limits GPIO/base current; Rpullup holds the
transistor off during reset. These do not replace the two LED resistors.
If the LEDs' positive leads are already joined, separate those leads to insert
one resistor per LED; join the two resistor inputs at the collector. Their
negative leads can remain joined to GND. This alternative uses individual
resistors for more predictable current sharing. To keep the existing LED
wiring intact, use the low-current shared-resistor option at the top instead.
E/B/C mean the actual emitter/base/collector pins. Verify the exact package's
pinout; do not assume a left-to-right order from the part number alone.

With a typical red LED drop near 2V, the example gives about 1mA per LED, so it
is a conservative starting point and may be dim compared with the original eyes.
Choose different LED resistors only after confirming the LEDs' specifications.
White/blue LEDs may have too little voltage headroom in this 3.3V circuit.

READING THE RESISTORS

For four bands, read from the end opposite the gold/silver tolerance band:
  brown-black-red-gold   = 1,000 ohms (1k), +/-5%
  brown-black-red-silver = 1,000 ohms (1k), +/-10%
The previously suspected blue band has been confirmed black; use the 1k value.
If unsure, measure resistance with power disconnected and the resistor removed
from the circuit (or with one end disconnected).
